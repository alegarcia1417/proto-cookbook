## HW3 Conclusion

Hurricane Helene was a devastating Category 4 hurricane that made landfall in Florida on September 25th, 2024. As the storm moved inland, it transitioned to an extratropical cyclone while stalling over the Tennessee Valley and the southern Appalachian Mountains. This stalling, coupled with strong winds, record-breaking amounts of rainfall, and moist antecedent conditions, led to disastrous impacts over this region. Notebook 1 and 2 both used data from the US Census Bureau to plot population, exposure, and recovery data from the Census Bureau’s Assessment, Recovery, and Evaluation datasets, or CAREs, over the affected counties during Hurricane Helene. Notebook 1 highlighted the counties that issued a presidential disaster declaration, as well as mapped power outages and maximum wind speeds. Notebook 2 mapped social vulnerability indices such as poverty and rurality within the impacted counties. These notebooks showed the spatial distribution of Helene's impacts in a multifaceted way, highlighting the socioeconomical context along with the immediate impacts of Helene. Approaching this disaster from a social vulnerability perspective helps provide understanding into why this disaster was as severe as it was through all phases of emergency management.
